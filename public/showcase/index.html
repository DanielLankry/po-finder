02 / UNDER THE SURFACE

The decisions behind
the interface.

Next.js 16 · React 19 · TypeScript
Supabase · HYP · Resend · Cloudflare

01

Payments survive the browser.

Provider verification determines settlement. Payment attempts snapshot the price and listing expiry, so late callbacks and refunds can be handled against the original transaction.

Trade-off: more explicit state and reconciliation, less dependence on a redirect succeeding.

02

Public listings, private ownership.

Column grants and row-level security separate public discovery from owner data. A dedicated owner RPC supports the dashboard; photos use short-lived signed URLs.

Trade-off: deliberate read models and URL renewal instead of exposing broad database rows.

03

Time follows the business.

One Israel-time resolver handles weekly hours, overnight openings, and dated location overrides. List and map share filtering and effective coordinates.

Trade-off: calendar-aware rules rather than treating every day as 24 elapsed hours.

04

Operations are part of the product.

A private support inbox, immutable email requests, provider idempotency keys, and an admin workspace support day-to-day operation. Payment notices use a database outbox.

Trade-off: durable queues and reconciliation rather than treating provider acceptance as delivery.

03 / DELIVERY & OWNERSHIP

Built, operated,
and wound down deliberately.

The repository includes native regression tests, PostgreSQL invariant tests, and Playwright coverage. Its wind-down preserves existing promotional periods, blocks new commitments at the application and database layers, and keeps this showcase independent of the live backend.

Designed and built a full-stack local-business discovery platform with Hebrew RTL UX, geospatial search, Supabase access controls, verified payment settlement, and durable transactional email workflows.

The demo uses a fixed fictional scenario. Provider round trips and production performance require separate evidence; no growth or revenue claims are made here.
